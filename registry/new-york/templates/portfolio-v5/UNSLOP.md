# Unslop

A pass to run against everything written during personalization — the
about paragraphs and every project `description` in `app/page.tsx`. This
applies whenever `AGENTS.md` has you drafting prose for this site.

Short copy is where AI tells are most obvious, and this template is almost
nothing but short copy. Hold it to a high bar.

## Process

1. Draft the content per `AGENTS.md`.
2. Before you're done, read it back against every pattern below.
3. Rewrite what trips a pattern. Preserve the meaning and the person's real
   voice — don't flatten it, just strip the tells.
4. Self-audit once more: "would this specific sentence survive if a human
   who does this work for real wrote it?" If not, cut or rewrite it.

## Patterns to detect and fix

### Style

1. **Em dashes.** This is short copy — use none. A single em dash in a
   two-sentence bio is the most common generated-bio tell there is. Use a
   period or a comma.
   - Bad: "Hey — I'm Jon. Fullstack developer — building for AI teams."
   - Better: "I'm Jon, a fullstack developer."
2. **Colon as a mid-sentence connector.** "The honest version:" or "The
   short story:" add nothing — they're a wind-up before the actual
   sentence. Cut the wind-up, state the point.
3. **Manufactured profundity.** A closing line that sounds like a fortune
   cookie is a tell, not a flourish.
   - Bad: "I build things I'd want to use. That's the whole philosophy."
   - Just say what the thing is: "A recipe box for the meals I actually
     cook."
4. **Rhetorical setups.** "X reads like Y until you realize Z" and similar
   setup-then-reveal openings are a generated pattern. Open with the fact.
5. **Title case, curly quotes, decorative emphasis.** Sentence case,
   straight quotes, no bolding proper nouns.

### Language

6. **AI vocabulary.** Additionally, crucial, delve, empower, facilitate,
   foster, garner, intricate, landscape (abstract), leverage, pivotal,
   robust, seamless, showcase, streamline, tapestry, testament,
   transformative, underscore, utilize, vibrant, "at the intersection of."
   Replace with the plain word or cut.
7. **Fancy ways to say "is."** "serves as", "stands as", "boasts". Just say
   "is" or "has."
8. **"Not just X, but Y."** State the point directly.
9. **Rule of three.** Two triads back to back — "simple, tested, and
   maintainable" then "cooking, running, and reading" — is a strong tell.
   Use the number of items that's actually true, and vary the sentence
   shape so it isn't three identical parallel clauses every time.
10. **Vague attribution.** "People say", "known for" — if there's no
    source, cut the claim or make it a fact you can stand behind.

### Length

11. **Padding past the point.** An extra clause restating the previous one,
    an implication spelled out that the reader already got. `AGENTS.md`
    gives a word-count budget measured against what you're replacing — if
    you're over it, cut a sentence, don't tighten the wording of all of
    them.
12. **One idea per paragraph.** If the two `details` paragraphs make the
    same point from different sides, keep the sharper one and cut the
    other, or make the second one do a different job (the demo's second
    paragraph is about life outside work, not more work philosophy).

### Voice

13. **Say what happened, not how it felt.** "made the workflow seamless"
    names a feeling. "cut the review step from three tools to one" names a
    fact. If a project description could sit unchanged in anyone else's
    portfolio, it says nothing — make it specific to this project.
14. **Active voice, real actor.** "the score was improved by streaming"
    becomes "streaming the response improved the score."
15. **Cut adverbs propping up a weak verb.** "runs significantly faster"
    becomes the actual number, or a stronger verb.
16. **Plain word over the fancier synonym.** "utilize" → "use", "leverage"
    → "use", "facilitate" → "help", "numerous" → "many."
17. **Chatbot artifacts.** No "I hope this helps," no apologizing, no
    address to a reader who isn't there — this is a bio, not a chat reply.

## Sound test

Read the about section out loud. If it sounds like something written
_about_ the person rather than _by_ them, it's still slopped.
