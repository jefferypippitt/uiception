Physics can sound like a museum of special cases: gravity here, electromagnetism there, quantum rules in a locked room at the back. I wrote *Why Anything Happens* to put the cases back into one story — how a handful of interactions, plus time, produce a universe that looks complicated from the inside.

I begin with motion and force in plain language, then widen to fields, probability, and the uncomfortable fact that “empty” is still governed. I do not pretend every reader wants the full mathematical machinery. I do insist that the machinery has a shape you can feel without a blackboard.

The chapters move from falling apples to orbiting moons to the statistical quiet of atoms that refuse classical certainty. The through-line is cause: what makes one outcome more likely than another, and why the sky we inherit is not arbitrary decoration but the residue of lawful processes.

Read it as a companion to the outer-system book if you want the *where*; read it alone if you want the *why*. Either way, my promise is the same as the rest of the public work — wonder attached to mechanism, sentences that stay true after the metaphor fades.
