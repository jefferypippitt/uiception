I became a physicist because a book made the night sky feel like a place you could *understand*, not only admire.

I no longer remember the edition. I remember the voice: patient, concrete, unwilling to replace a hard idea with a metaphor and walk away. It treated the universe as something that could be explained to a curious person without insulting them, and without pretending that explanation is the same as poetry.

After that, classroom physics stopped feeling like a closed club. Vectors were a way to talk about motion without waving your hands. Spectra were a way to ask a star what it was made of. Orbits stopped looking like decoration.

I still write for that reader: someone who looked up, got hooked, and wanted the next sentence to be true. Public science is the same subject, spoken in a register that does not require a degree to enter the room.

If you want a place to start among my notes, begin with [RIP Pluto](/writing/rip-pluto).
