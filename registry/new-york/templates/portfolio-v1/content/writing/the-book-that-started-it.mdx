I did not become a physicist because someone handed me an equation. I became one because a book made the night sky feel like a place you could *understand* — not only admire.

The details of which edition and which shelf do not matter much. What mattered was the voice: patient, concrete, unwilling to replace a hard idea with a metaphor and walk away. It treated the universe as something that could be explained to a curious person without insulting them, and without pretending that explanation is the same as poetry.

After that, classroom physics stopped feeling like a closed club. Vectors were a way to talk about motion without waving your hands. Spectra were a way to ask a star what it was made of. Orbits were not circles drawn for decoration — they were consequences.

I still write for that reader: someone who looked up, got hooked, and wanted the next sentence to be true. Public science is not a lesser form of research. It is the same subject, spoken in a register that does not require a degree to enter the room.

If you are looking for a place to start with my own notes, begin with [RIP Pluto](/writing/rip-pluto). The universe does not need defending. It needs translating.
