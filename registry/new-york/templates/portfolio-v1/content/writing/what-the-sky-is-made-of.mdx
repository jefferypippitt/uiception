On a clear night the sky looks like a black sheet with lights stuck to it. That is a useful lie, and I still catch myself believing it for a second. The black is a long look through vacuum — and vacuum is not empty the way a drawer is empty. It is a place where light can travel for years without hitting much of anything.

What you see is almost entirely photons. Some left a nearby star minutes or years ago. Some bounced off the Moon. A few are the washed-out glow of our own atmosphere scattering sunlight long after sunset. The points are sources. The darkness between them is the path.

There is more in that darkness than eyes report: magnetic fields, sparse gas, dust, neutrinos streaming through everything including you. None of that paints the sky blue at noon or black at midnight. The color of night is too little scattered light to overwhelm the retina. The physics is still there.

I keep wonder attached to mechanism. The sky is radiation and geometry. Constellations are mnemonic overlays. And “empty” is a volume so thin that a photon can travel for years and still arrive.
